Lesson Introduction

So far your listing has been one page for everyone. But the people who reach your app are not all the same. A student in Ibadan who clicked a school-newsletter link, a parent in Accra who searched "school fees tracker", and a lapsed user you want to win back each care about different things. Custom store listings (CSLs) let you show different versions of your Google Play page to different audiences, with a different message, screenshots and description for each.

This lesson is Module 4: Drive user acquisition through custom store listings of the Google Play Store Listing Certificate (https://playacademy.withgoogle.com/certificate), followed by practical guidance on preparing for the certificate exam. We teach the skills in our own words with our own examples. We have not seen the exam, we cannot reproduce it, and nothing here guarantees a pass.

Honesty note. Custom store listings are a changing feature: limits on the number of listings, targeting options and Console menu names are updated from time to time, and some options depend on your account or app. Where we quote a number, treat it as "at the time of writing" and check the current Play Console Help. Exam format, number of attempts, fees and renewal rules are set by Google Play Academy: read them on the certificate page, not from us.

By the end you will be able to:

  • Explain what a custom store listing is and when it is worth the effort.
  • Name the targeting options: country or region, user state (new, lapsed or returning), pre-registration, specific URLs and campaigns, and Google Ads.
  • Plan a set of custom listings from an audience map.
  • Create a custom listing in Play Console and link to it with the right URL.
  • Combine custom listings with tracking and experiments to measure their effect.
  • Avoid the policy mistakes specific to personalised listings.
  • Build a study plan for the certificate exam and check your readiness with self-made practice questions.

Big idea: a custom listing is a tailored welcome. Everything in it must still be true, but it can lead with the aspect of your app that matters most to that visitor.

Conceptual Understanding: Why Different Pages for Different People?

Consider a shop on a busy road in Ibadan. A customer coming off a bus wants to know whether you sell phone chargers. A neighbour who comes every week wants to hear about the new stock. A person who has not visited for a year wants a reason to come back. A good shopkeeper greets each differently, and does not lie to any of them.

A custom store listing works the same way. It is an alternative version of your listing page that Google Play shows to a particular audience.

text
                      +-----------------------------------+
                      |  Default (main) store listing     |  everyone else
                      +-----------------------------------+
                         ^                   ^
  Google Play shows the best match for the visitor:
                         |                   |
   +---------------------+----+   +----------+----------------------+
   | Custom listing A         |   | Custom listing B                |
   | Ghana, English           |   | Lapsed users (not opened in a   |
   | cedi, mobile money story |   | while): "What's new: reports"   |
   +--------------------------+   +---------------------------------+
   +--------------------------+   +---------------------------------+
   | Custom listing C         |   | Custom listing D                |
   | URL for school newsletter|   | URL for Google Ads campaign     |
   | "Free for your school"   |   | matching the ad promise         |
   +--------------------------+   +---------------------------------+

What can differ between listings?

Typically you can vary the title? No: the app name and icon stay the same in custom listings (check the current rules), while you can change elements such as the short and full descriptions, the feature graphic, screenshots and promo video. Custom listings are about the message, not a different identity. Check the Help page for the exact list of changeable fields.

Why they work (and when they do not)

  • Relevance raises conversion. A visitor who clicked an ad about "offline attendance" and lands on a page that leads with offline attendance sees the promise kept. This is called message match.
  • Re-engagement. A user who left may return if the page shows what is new since they left.
  • Pre-registration and launches. A custom listing can prepare early adopters.
  • Effort versus reward. Every custom listing needs assets, translation, review and maintenance. If you have a handful of visitors, the effort is wasted. They pay off where you have meaningful traffic in a distinct segment that you can describe in one sentence.

Reported averages from Google (for example the figure in the 2023 course announcement on the Android Developers Blog) show positive effects on average, but your results will differ: test.

Step-by-Step Build 1: The Targeting Options

When you create a custom listing in the Console you choose who sees it. The exact options change; these are the main ideas.

Option Who sees it Example use
Country / region Visitors in the chosen countries Local currency, local examples, a localised message for Ghana vs Nigeria
User state New users, lapsed users (previously installed or engaged, now inactive), or returning users A "welcome back, see what changed" listing
Pre-registration People who pre-registered before launch A "thanks for waiting, it's live" message
URL (custom link) Anyone who arrives through a specific URL you create A school newsletter, a QR code on a poster, a partner site
Google Ads People who click a specific Google Ads campaign (app campaigns for Google Play) Landing-page match to the ad
Channels where you share links Social posts, influencers, email Different URLs per channel, so you can measure each

A few rules that usually apply (verify in Help):

  • Each custom listing has one targeting type or combination allowed by the Console, and when more than one listing matches a visitor, a documented priority decides which is shown.
  • Listings targeting a country or user state are shown automatically to those users in places such as Google Play search and Explore. Listings targeting URLs are only shown to those who open that URL.
  • There is a limit on how many custom listings an app can have, and that limit can differ by account type.
  • A custom listing cannot be shown if the app is not published in the targeted country, or if the listing fails review.

Step 2: Prioritise

Score each audience from 1 to 5 on size, message difference (how different the best message is from your default) and effort, then pick the top two or three.

text
        Size  Difference  Effort(low=5)  Total
A        3        3            5           11
B        5        4            3           12   <- do first

Step 3: Write a brief for each

text
CUSTOM LISTING B: Ghana
Audience: visitors in Ghana (search + explore)
Goal: raise CVR from 9% (default) toward the Nigeria level (18%)
Hypothesis: showing cedi in screenshots and mentioning mobile money exports will increase trust
Changed fields: short description, full description (first paragraph), screenshots 1-3, feature graphic
Same: app name, icon, requirements, contact
Success metric: store listing CVR for Ghana visitors; compare with the previous 4 weeks
Review date: after 3 weeks

Step 4: Check truthfulness

Every claim in a custom listing must be true for that user and that app version. If the Ghana listing says "pay with mobile money", the app must really support it for users in Ghana. A custom listing that promises more than the app offers is a policy violation and a reason for one-star reviews.

Step-by-Step Build 3: Creating a Custom Listing (the Workflow)

The Console flow below is approximate; use the Help article "Create custom store listings" for the current screens.

  1. Go to Grow users → Store presence → Custom store listings (or the equivalent).
  2. Click Create listing. Give it an internal name only you see: GH-search-v1 (see naming below).
  3. Choose the targeting: for example Country, and select Ghana.
  4. Edit the content for each language you serve: short description, full description, graphics, screenshots, video.
  5. Preview how it looks.
  6. Save and publish the listing. It goes through review. Custom listing changes can be reviewed separately from app releases.
  7. For URL-targeted listings, copy the listing URL. It is the normal Play link plus a parameter that identifies the listing. Use that URL in newsletters, posters (as a QR code), social posts or ad campaigns. Never guess or hand-edit the identifier; copy it from the Console.
  8. Wait for traffic, then read the report (below).

Naming convention

A naming scheme makes reports readable six months later:

text
<AUDIENCE>-<CHANNEL>-<LANG>-v<N>

GH-search-en-v1       Ghana, organic search, English
NG-newsletter-en-v2   Nigeria school newsletter link, second version
LAPSED-all-en-v1      Lapsed users, all countries, English
ADS-offline-en-v3     Google Ads campaign "offline", third creative

Linking to a listing from a web page

For URL-targeted listings, you place the link on a page or in a QR code. A plain HTML button works:

html
<a href="https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.techbase.classregister&listing=ng-newsletter-en-v2">
  Get Class Register on Google Play
</a>

The listing= parameter here shows the idea of a listing identifier; the actual parameter name and value come from the Console's generated URL, so always copy that URL. You can make a QR code from the link with a free generator, or in Python with the qrcode library (pip install qrcode[pil]):

python
import qrcode

url = "PASTE_THE_URL_FROM_PLAY_CONSOLE_HERE"
img = qrcode.make(url)
img.save("ng-newsletter-qr.png")
print("saved ng-newsletter-qr.png")

Test the QR code on a real phone in the actual printed size and light before you print a thousand copies.

Step-by-Step Build 4: Measuring Custom Listings

Without measurement, custom listings are decoration. The store listing acquisition report (lesson 51) can be filtered by custom store listing, so you can compare:

Metric Compare
Visitors, installers, conversion Custom listing vs the default for the same country/source and period
Retained installers (day 1, 7, 30) Do the visitors the custom message attracted stay?
Ratings of new installers Do expectations match reality?

A comparison helper

Using the two-proportion test from lesson 51, a small function can say whether a custom listing's conversion is credibly different from the default over the same period:

python
from math import sqrt, erf

def compare(label, v_default, i_default, v_custom, i_custom):
    pa, pb = i_default / v_default, i_custom / v_custom
    pooled = (i_default + i_custom) / (v_default + v_custom)
    se = sqrt(pooled * (1 - pooled) * (1 / v_default + 1 / v_custom))
    z = (pb - pa) / se
    p = 2 * (1 - 0.5 * (1 + erf(abs(z) / sqrt(2))))
    verdict = "evidence of a difference" if p < 0.05 else "not enough evidence"
    print(f"{label}: default {pa:.1%}, custom {pb:.1%}, p={p:.3f} -> {verdict}")

compare("Ghana search", 3000, 270, 3200, 400)
compare("Newsletter",    600, 168,  650, 190)

Output:
Ghana search: default 9.0%, custom 12.5%, p=0.000 -> evidence of a difference
Newsletter: default 28.0%, custom 29.2%, p=0.631 -> not enough evidence

(The first comparison, 9.0% to 12.5% over thousands of visitors, is clearly a real gain; the second, 28.0% to 29.2% on a few hundred visitors, could easily be luck.)

Caveat: custom listings and the default do not see randomised traffic the way an experiment does; the audiences differ (that is the point). A difference may come from the audience, not the page. To isolate the effect of wording, run a store listing experiment on the custom listing's elements where the Console allows it (lesson 51).

Close the loop

After a month, update the decision log: did the listing meet its goal? If yes, extend (more countries, new audience). If not, was it the message, the assets or the audience? Retire listings that do not earn their upkeep. Many small listings can become a maintenance burden whenever the app UI changes.

Step-by-Step Build 5: Policy and Good Practice for Custom Listings
  • Truth: every claim must be accurate for those users and the app they will install.
  • Same app: a custom listing cannot promise a different app or hide important facts (price, subscription, data practices).
  • Sensitive targeting: target by country, activity state and campaign, not by sensitive personal attributes. Do not use text that singles out users by health, religion, race or similar attributes in a way policy forbids.
  • Do not impersonate another developer, or imply endorsement.
  • Keep assets in sync: screenshots must match the current UI.
  • Children's apps and sensitive categories have stricter listing rules.
  • Accessibility: captions and graphics still need good contrast (lesson 47).
  • Consistency of the in-app experience: if a listing promises a feature, the first-run experience should help the user find it.

The course was first launched in 2021 and updated in 2023 with new modules on custom store listings and listing experiments, so check the current version.

Exam technique

  • Read each question twice; look for words such as "best", "first", "NOT".
  • Eliminate clearly wrong options (those that break policy or promise guaranteed results).
  • Prefer answers that are honest, user-focused and measurable. In this subject, the correct answer is rarely "stuff keywords", "fake reviews" or "change everything at once".
  • Do not rush, but do not stall: flag and return.
  • Know the numbers that matter from the Console (field lengths, image sizes) but verify them against the current Console, because exams are written from current guidance.
  • Rest the night before; this is an understanding test, not a memory marathon.

After the exam

If you pass: add the certificate to your CV and LinkedIn with the date, and keep note of the renewal period on the certificate page. If you do not: use the feedback, review the weak modules and retake after any waiting period the rules require. Either way, the real goal is to run better listings, and you have the tools to do that.

Guided Practice Exercises
🎯 Your Challenge

Exercise 1: Build an audience map

For an app you know (or Market Price Check), list four audiences, the message for each and whether a custom listing or the default listing is the right place to serve them. Choose your first two to build and justify.

Example for Market Price Check: (1) Lagos shoppers: default listing (the largest group, message already general). (2) Visitors from a partner traders' association link: URL-targeted custom listing, message "Prices reported by traders you know", assets showing the association name only if you have permission. (3) Users in Ghana: country-targeted custom listing, cedi prices and Kumasi/Accra markets in screenshots. (4) Lapsed users: lapsed-user custom listing, "New: price alerts" with screenshots of the alert feature. First two to build: Ghana (large traffic, clear message difference) and partner link (high trust, small effort, easy to measure). Justification: highest size-times-difference score for the lowest effort; the lapsed listing waits until the alert feature has been released and the audience is large enough to measure.

🎯 Your Challenge

Exercise 2: Truth audit

A custom listing for "Lapsed users" says: "Come back! Now with AI grading and free premium forever." The app has no AI grading, and premium costs money after 30 days. List what is wrong and rewrite the opening.

Problems: claims a feature that does not exist (misleading), "free premium forever" contradicts the 30-day trial (deceptive about price), and no attention to the user's likely reason for leaving. Rewrite: Welcome back! Since you last used Class Register we added weekly reports you can share as PDF, large text support and faster loading. Try it free; premium features are optional and start after your 30-day trial. Every claim is true, the price is clear and the message is specific to returning users.

🎯 Your Challenge

Exercise 3: Decide

The data: default Ghana listing, 2,000 visitors, 180 installers; custom Ghana listing, 2,100 visitors, 205 installers over the same four weeks. Is the custom listing better? What would you do next?

Default CVR 9.0%; custom 9.76%. That is a 0.76-point gain, but with these visitor numbers the difference is not statistically convincing (the z-test p-value is around 0.4). Conclusion: not enough evidence; do not declare victory. Next: keep it running longer to collect more visitors, make the message more distinct (a bolder change such as showing mobile-money export in screenshot 1), check retention and ratings for the custom listing's installers, and compare against a localised experiment for a cleaner test.

Phase 11 of 12
Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

Mistake #1: Making many listings with tiny differences. Symptom: heavy maintenance, no measurable result. Fix: only audiences with a clearly different message and enough traffic.

Mistake #2: Not copying the URL from the Console. Symptom: a link that shows the default listing. Fix: use the generated URL.

Mistake #3: Listings that over-promise. Fix: every claim true for that audience.

Mistake #4: Forgetting to update screenshots after a release. Fix: add listing review to the release checklist.

Mistake #5: Reading the comparison without a significance check. Fix: the helper or the Console's intervals.

Mistake #6: No naming convention. Fix: audience, channel, language, version.

Mistake #7: Sending a print campaign to an untested QR code. Fix: test on real phones.

Mistake #8: Studying only the exam. Fix: practise on real listings.

Mistake #9: Counting on the certificate to get installs. Fix: the certificate shows knowledge; installs come from a good app and listing.

Mistake #10: Quoting old limits. Fix: confirm in the Console.

Phase 12 of 12
Knowledge Check

Question 1. What is a custom store listing?

An alternative version of your store listing (different descriptions, graphics, screenshots or video) shown to a targeted audience such as users in a country, lapsed users or people arriving by a specific URL.

Question 2. Give three ways you can target a custom listing.

Any three of: country or region, user state (new, lapsed, returning), pre-registration, a specific URL or campaign link, a Google Ads campaign.

Question 3. Why does "message match" improve conversion?

When the landing page continues the promise of the ad or link the visitor clicked, the visitor immediately sees what they came for, so more of them install.

Question 4. How do you tell whether a custom listing really helps?

Compare conversion and retention against the default for the same period, source and country, check the difference with a significance test or the Console's intervals, and use experiments where possible because audiences differ.

Question 5. What is the best way to prepare for the certificate exam?

Complete the official course modules, practise the skills on real listings and data, learn the vocabulary, review current Console limits and policies, and read the exam rules on the official certificate page.
