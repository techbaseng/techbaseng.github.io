On 6 December 2023, Google announced Gemini, a family of AI models in three sizes: Ultra for the most complex tasks, Pro for a wide range of tasks and Nano for use directly on phones. Google described it as "natively multimodal".

## What is multimodal?

Earlier systems often handled one type of data: text or images or sound. A multimodal model is trained to work with several types together. You might show it a photograph of a maths problem and ask for an explanation, or give it a chart and ask what it shows.

## The wider picture

By the end of 2023, there were many strong AI models: OpenAI's GPT-4, Google's Gemini, Anthropic's Claude, Meta's open Llama models and others. They competed on accuracy, speed, price and safety. That competition is good for users, but it also means the tools and their abilities keep changing.

## How to cope with so much change

- **Learn the ideas, not only the buttons.** Prompts, checking and understanding data stay useful whichever tool you use.
- **Try more than one.** Different tools suit different jobs.
- **Keep a human in charge.** The person using the tool is responsible for the result.
