Most online attacks do not need clever hacking. They rely on people being rushed or careless. These five habits stop a great many of them.

## 1. Use long, different passwords
A long phrase of random words is stronger than a short, complicated one. Use a different password for every important account, and keep them in a password manager if you can.

## 2. Turn on two-step verification
This adds a second check, such as a code sent to your phone, so that a stolen password alone is not enough. Switch it on for email, social media and banking first.

## 3. Pause before you click
Fake messages pretend to come from banks, delivery firms or even friends. If a message is urgent, offers money or asks for a code or password, stop and check through another route.

## 4. Keep devices updated
Updates fix weaknesses that criminals know about. Switch on automatic updates for phones and computers.

## 5. Talk about it
Children should feel they can tell an adult when something strange happens, without fear of blame.

## For schools

Include a short lesson on safe online behaviour each term. It takes little time and protects pupils, teachers and school records. Techbase can help design one for your school.
