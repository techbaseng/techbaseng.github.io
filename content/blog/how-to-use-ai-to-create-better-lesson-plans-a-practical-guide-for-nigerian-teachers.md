Most teachers have the same disappointing first experience with AI. They type something short, like "write a lesson plan on photosynthesis", and receive something generic that does not fit their curriculum and is barely quicker than writing it themselves. Usually the tool is fine. The prompt is the problem.

These four steps fix it.

## Step 1: Give the AI the context it needs

At minimum, say which subject and topic, which class, which curriculum (WAEC, NECO, the British curriculum or your own scheme of work), how long the lesson is, and what students should be able to do by the end.

Compare:

- **Weak:** "Write a lesson plan on photosynthesis."
- **Strong:** "Write a 40-minute lesson note on photosynthesis for JSS2 students following the Nigerian basic science curriculum. Learning objectives: students should be able to define photosynthesis, state the chemical equation, and identify the raw materials and products. Include an introduction or hook, main teaching content, a class activity and three assessment questions."

The second takes thirty extra seconds to write and gives you something that needs light editing instead of a rewrite.

## Step 2: Ask for a structure

Say how the output should be organised. For example: "Structure this as: Introduction, Objectives, Materials Needed, Lesson Development (with timings), Assessment, Take-home Assignment." You will get something close to your usual lesson note format rather than a wall of text to reformat.

## Step 3: Do the same for assessments

Be exact about how many questions, what type (multiple choice, theory, fill in the blank), how hard, and whether you want an answer key.

> "Generate 15 multiple-choice questions on photosynthesis for JSS2, with four options each and one correct answer. Put the answer key at the end."

That gets you close to an exam-ready draft in a single pass.

## Step 4: Always check before you use it

This is the step teachers are most tempted to skip, and the most important. AI can sound confident while being wrong, aim at the wrong level, or miss your syllabus. Treat the output like a draft from a capable colleague you do not know yet. Read all of it, correct what is wrong, and only then take it into the classroom.

## A template to start with today

Copy this and replace the brackets:

> "Act as an experienced [subject] teacher in Nigeria. Write a [duration]-minute lesson note on [topic] for [class level], following the [curriculum name] curriculum. Learning objectives: [list them]. Structure the note with an introduction, main content, a classroom activity and three assessment questions with an answer key."
