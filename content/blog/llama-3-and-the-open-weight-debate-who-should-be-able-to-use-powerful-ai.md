On 18 April 2024, Meta released Llama 3, a family of language models whose "weights", the numbers that make up the trained model, could be downloaded and used by developers, subject to a licence. Some people call such releases open-weight. They are not always "open source" in the strict sense, because training data and some restrictions are not shared.

## The case for open models

- **Access.** Students, start-ups and researchers who cannot pay for large cloud services can run and study the models.
- **Transparency.** More people can inspect how the models behave.
- **Local solutions.** Developers can adapt a model to local languages and needs, which matters for African languages that are poorly served by big products.

## The case for caution

- **Misuse.** Once released, a model cannot easily be recalled, and safeguards can be removed.
- **Responsibility.** It is harder to hold anyone accountable when a model is modified and spread widely.

## Where we stand

There is no simple answer, and thoughtful experts disagree. For learners, the useful point is that AI is not only a product from a few companies. Understanding how models are built, trained and shared opens a path to becoming creators of technology, not only users.
