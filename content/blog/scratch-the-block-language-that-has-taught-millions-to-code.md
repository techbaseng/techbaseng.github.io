Scratch was created by the Lifelong Kindergarten group at the MIT Media Lab and released in 2007. It is free, runs in a web browser and is used by tens of millions of young people around the world.

Instead of typing commands, learners drag blocks together like puzzle pieces. A block that says "move 10 steps" fits under one that says "when green flag clicked", and a cartoon character walks across the screen. Mistakes are visible and easy to fix, so children feel brave about trying things.

## The ideas hidden inside the play

- **Sequences:** doing things in the right order.
- **Loops:** repeating an action without writing it again and again.
- **Conditions:** "if the cat touches the wall, then turn around".
- **Variables:** remembering a score or a name.
- **Events:** reacting when someone clicks or presses a key.

These are the same ideas that appear in Python, JavaScript and every other language. A child who is comfortable with them in Scratch is not starting from zero when text-based code arrives.
