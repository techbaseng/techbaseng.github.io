When COVID-19 closed schools and workplaces in early 2020, millions of people learned new tools in a matter of weeks: video calls, online classrooms, mobile payments, delivery apps and shared documents. By mid-2022, many restrictions had eased, but much of that change had become permanent.

## What stuck

- **Video meetings and remote work.** Many organisations now mix office and home working, and hire people across cities and countries.
- **Digital payments and online shopping.** Habits formed during lockdowns continued.
- **Blended learning.** Recorded lessons, online quizzes and shared notes are now normal parts of many schools.
- **Telehealth.** Remote consultations became far more common.

## What we learned

The pandemic also exposed a digital divide. Learners with a laptop, a steady connection and a quiet space could carry on. Others fell behind, and in Nigeria, where data costs, power cuts and shared devices are everyday realities, that gap was very visible.

## What it means for us

Tools that work offline, lessons that run on modest devices and teachers who feel confident with technology all make schools more resilient. That is one reason we built our Academy to be free and light enough to use on ordinary laptops, and why we train teachers as well as pupils.

Technology did not replace teachers during the lockdowns. It showed how much a good teacher, with the right tools, matters.
