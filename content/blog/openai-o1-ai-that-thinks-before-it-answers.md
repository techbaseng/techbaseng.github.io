On 12 September 2024, OpenAI released o1-preview and o1-mini, models trained to work through a problem step by step before giving an answer. OpenAI reported much stronger results on difficult maths, science and programming questions than earlier models.

## What is different

Standard language models respond almost immediately. Reasoning models first produce an internal chain of thought, trying approaches and checking them, and then give a final answer. This takes longer and costs more computing power, but it can solve harder problems. Other companies have since introduced similar reasoning models.

## Why this is useful for learning

- **Showing the working.** For maths and coding, the steps matter as much as the answer.
- **Harder questions.** Multi-step problems that stumped earlier models become more manageable.

## What has not changed

Reasoning models can still be wrong. A longer explanation can sound convincing even when a step is mistaken. So the human still needs to understand the problem well enough to check.

## What we teach

We ask learners to write down their plan before they write code. Breaking a problem into steps is the very skill that reasoning models imitate, and it is one that no tool can do for you if you want to truly understand.
